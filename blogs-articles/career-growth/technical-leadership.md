# Technical Leadership

## Overview

Technical leadership is the ability to guide engineering teams toward technical excellence without relying on managerial authority. It's about influence, vision, and enabling others to do their best work.

## Leading Without Authority

### Build Trust
- Deliver on commitments consistently
- Admit mistakes and learn from them
- Support team members in public, critique in private

### Influence Through Expertise
- Develop deep knowledge in your domain
- Share knowledge through tech talks, docs, and mentoring
- Be the go-to person for hard problems

### Communicate Vision
- Articulate the "why" behind technical decisions
- Connect technical work to business outcomes
- Make complex ideas accessible to non-technical stakeholders

## Decision-Making Framework

### Data-Driven Decisions
- Collect metrics before and after changes
- Use A/B tests to validate hypotheses
- Present data, not opinions, in discussions

### Consensus Building
- **Inform**: Share information and gather input
- **Consult**: Seek feedback from stakeholders
- **Decide**: Make the call with available information
- **Communicate**: Explain the decision and rationale

### Documenting Decisions
- Use **ADRs** (Architecture Decision Records)
- Record context, options, decision, and consequences
- Review and update decisions as circumstances change

## Mentoring and Growth

### Coaching Approach
- Ask questions instead of giving answers
- Help engineers develop problem-solving skills
- Provide specific, actionable feedback

### Creating Growth Opportunities
- Assign stretch projects that build new skills
- Pair junior engineers with senior mentors
- Advocate for team members in promotion reviews

## Staff-Level Insights

### The Servant Leader Model
- Remove blockers for your team
- Shield the team from unnecessary meetings and context switching
- Invest in tooling and infrastructure that makes developers more productive
- **Staff insight**: Your success is measured by your team's output, not your individual contributions. The best technical leaders make their teams look good.

### Managing Up
- Understand your manager's goals and pressures
- Proactively communicate progress and risks
- Bring solutions, not just problems
- **Staff insight**: Managing up isn't politics — it's ensuring your leadership understands and supports your team's technical direction. Schedule regular 1:1s with your manager to align on priorities.

### Handling Conflict
- Separate people from problems — critique ideas, not individuals
- Find common ground before discussing differences
- Escalate only when stuck, with clear context and proposed solutions
- **Staff insight**: Technical disagreements are healthy. They lead to better decisions. Your job as a leader is to create a safe environment for disagreement and guide the team to a decision — not to avoid conflict.
